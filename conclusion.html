How Discovery Methods of Exoplanets Relate to Our Understanding of Their Features

Conclusion

This project arrives at the realization that the discovery method plays an important role in how exoplanet traits are captured in the observations. Instead of providing a representative sample of exoplanets with similar characteristics, each method tends to detect a specific type of planet based on its properties and hence creates a biased sample of the exoplanets. With the emphasis on transits in the data set as well as the differences in the masses and orbital periods of the planets discovered using imaging, astrometry, microlensing, and radial velocity, it becomes clear that the exoplanet sample that we currently know is not only dependent on the variety in exoplanetary population itself, but also on the capability of our telescopes to discover different planets. Any attempt to uncover any patterns concerning the global characteristics of exoplanets should take into consideration the effect of the method used. Future studies should extend their investigations further to include other planetary and stellar traits, consider possible changes in time with respect to method-biased data samples, and conduct more comparisons between observatories.
